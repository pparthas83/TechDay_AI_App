# Consolidated Edison: Grid Resilience, Storm Modeling & Weather Readiness

## Storm Hardening & Climate Resilience Plan
Consolidated Edison operates one of the most reliable and resilient energy distribution systems in the world, serving dense urban and suburban regions. In response to extreme weather patterns (hurricanes, heat waves, nor'easters, and flash flooding), Con Edison has executed multi-billion-dollar grid hardening initiatives.

### 1. Advanced Severe Weather Modeling & AI
- **Microclimate Forecast Models**: High-resolution atmospheric modeling systems integrate real-time weather radar, wind gust tracking, soil moisture saturation levels, and tree canopy health.
- **Outage Prediction Engine**: Machine learning algorithms analyze predicted wind velocity against feeder topography to forecast potential line faults and customer outages up to 72 hours before storm landfall.
- **Proactive Resource Staging**: AI simulations optimize the prepositioning of utility repair trucks, tree-trimming contractors, and emergency mutual-aid utility crews in predicted impact zones.

### 2. Automated FLISR (Fault Location, Isolation, and Service Restoration)
- **Smart Grid Switches**: Advanced reclosers, automated sectionalizers, and motorized underground switches installed across overhead and underground distribution networks.
- **Sub-Minute Restoration**: In the event of a tree limb strike or cable fault, automated software isolates the damaged line segment and reroutes power through adjacent feeders in under 60 seconds, restoring electricity to the majority of affected customers before a truck even rolls.

### 3. Substation Flood Protection & Coastal Hardening
- **Perimeter Flood Walls & Surge Gates**: Construction of reinforced concrete perimeter walls, flood gates, and high-capacity submersible pumps protecting critical substations along the East River, Hudson River, and coastal zones.
- **Elevated Control Equipment**: Control rooms, SCADA telemetry cabinets, and protective relay systems elevated above FEMA 100-year and 500-year flood levels plus sea-level rise margins.
